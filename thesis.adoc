The methods section should explain how the investigation was carried out. Describe the materials, assumptions, and procedures in enough detail for the reader to assess the results. State which choices were made before collecting evidence and which arose during the analysis. Where a procedure builds on published work, cite the relevant source and explain any changes made for this study.

Present the results separately from the interpretation when that distinction helps the reader follow the argument. Explain what each observation shows and how it relates to the original research question. Do not replace missing measurements with illustrative values or describe a proposed test as if it had already been performed. Discuss uncertainty where it affects the conclusions, and make clear which findings are directly supported by the evidence. These sample paragraphs provide enough continuous prose to demonstrate how the document flows between lists, headings, and subsequent sections. They should be replaced before the thesis is submitted.

A discussion can compare the findings with previous work and examine possible explanations for differences. Consider alternative interpretations and describe the limitations of the methods. Explain which conclusions remain valid within those limits and which questions require further investigation. Keep the distinction between observation and inference clear throughout the chapter. The purpose of the discussion is to connect the evidence to the research question, not to repeat every result. End with a transition that helps the reader understand why the next section follows from the argument presented here.

== Conclusions

Summarise the contribution of the work without introducing new evidence that has not been discussed in the preceding chapters. State the limits of the conclusions and identify possible directions for future investigation. Distinguish work that has been completed from work that is only proposed.
